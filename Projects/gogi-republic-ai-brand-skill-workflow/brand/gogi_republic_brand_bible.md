# Gogi Republic, Brand Bible

## Company Snapshot
Gogi Republic is a Korean BBQ chain based in Los Angeles, founded in 2019 by Min-Jae "MJ" Park. It has five locations: Koreatown, Sawtelle, Silver Lake, Long Beach, and Pasadena. Guests grill at the table, order rounds of meat and banchan, and stay longer than they planned.

## Founder
- **Min-Jae "MJ" Park**, 36, Korean American restaurant operator
- Grew up helping at his parents' strip-mall restaurant in Gardena
- Loves a loud dining room, a clean grill, and servers who know when to drop the next plate
- Hates stiff restaurant copy, fake "authenticity," and anything that sounds like a luxury lounge

## Restaurants
- Five locations across Los Angeles
- Service style is fast, warm, direct, and a little chaotic in the best way
- The room should feel like friends eating after a long week

## Mission
Make Korean BBQ feel like the best part of the night: hot grill, cold drinks, too much banchan, and a table that keeps passing plates.

## Voice and Tone
- Hungry, playful, communal, and direct
- Sounds like MJ talking to regulars, not a polished brand deck
- Uses sensory detail: smoke, char, sizzle, crunch, cold glass, hot grill
- Confident without being fancy
- Cheeky only when it earns the laugh

## Sample Voice
**Yes:**
> "Bring the group that says they are just doing one round. We both know how this ends."

> "The grill is hot, the kimchi is loud, and the first pour is already sweating on the table."

> "Koreatown gets first smoke Thursday night. Come hungry and wear the jacket that can handle it."

**No:**
> "Join us for an elevated culinary journey through premium Korean flavors."

> "Experience authentic fusion cuisine in a luxurious atmosphere."

## Signature Phrase
> "Grill loud, eat louder."

Use it once per piece at most. It works as a closing line, flyer footer, or landing page tag. Do not use it in every paragraph.

## Typography
- **Headings:** bold condensed sans, tight and loud
- **Body:** clean sans, readable, unfussy
- **Accents:** monospace or label-style caps for menu tags and location stamps

## Vocabulary Preferences
**Use these:**
- "grill," "sizzle," "char," "smoke," "banchan," "ssamjang," "galbi," "pork belly," "short rib," "table," "crew," "round two," "cold glass"
- "bring your people," "pass the tongs," "save room," "the grill is already hot"

**Avoid these:**
- "luxury," "premium," "elevated," "curated," "bespoke"
- "authentic journey," "fusion experience," "culinary destination"
- Over-explaining Korean food to the audience
- Forced slang, fake hype, and generic nightlife copy

## Menu Anchors
- **Meats:** galbi, pork belly, brisket, spicy chicken, ribeye cap
- **Banchan:** kimchi, pickled daikon, cucumber muchim, bean sprouts, scallion salad
- **Sauces:** ssamjang, sesame salt, gochujang glaze
- **Drinks:** soju, makgeolli, iced barley tea, cold beer

## Customer Persona
- Groups of friends eating after work or before a night out
- Families that know their favorite banchan order by heart
- Teams booking offsites who want energy without a banquet-hall feeling
- Food lovers who care about the grill, not white-tablecloth service

## Do's
- Write like MJ is talking to regulars
- Use one concrete food or table detail in every piece
- Keep lines short and punchy
- Let the room feel loud and social
- Mention the specific location when it matters

## Don'ts
- No luxury restaurant language
- No fake "discover Korea" copy
- No overusing Korean terms just to sound branded
- No exclamation marks unless the piece truly needs one
- No long paragraphs in promotional work

## Reusable Phrases
- "Grill loud, eat louder."
- "Pass the tongs."
- "Round two is not optional."
- "Wear the jacket that can handle smoke."
- "The grill is already hot."
- "Bring your people. We will bring the banchan."
